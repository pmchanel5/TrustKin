# Brotherhood MVP Market Notes

## What already exists

- HabitShare is a social habit tracker with friend accountability and per-friend privacy controls: https://habitshareapp.com/
- BeReal proves demand for seeing what friends are actually doing, but it is a broad photo social network, not an accountability circle: https://bereal.com/
- Friend Controls and similar iOS apps use real friends to approve or deny extra screen time. This is stronger control than Brotherhood should use for the MVP: https://apps.apple.com/us/app/friend-controls-screen-time/id6651826679
- ActivityWatch is a local-first automated time tracker for apps and websites. It is private and useful, but not built around encouragement from trusted friends: https://activitywatch.net/
- Accountable2You and Ever Accountable already share detailed device activity with accountability partners: https://accountable2you.com/features/ and https://everaccountable.com/how-it-works/
- Covenant Eyes uses screen accountability, including regular screenshots that are processed and shared in a blurred form: https://support.covenanteyes.com/hc/en-us/articles/11976472404763-How-does-monitoring-work
- Men's wellness/community apps exist, such as HeChangedIt, with peer chat, journaling, and mental fitness content: https://hechangedit.com/

## Why similar ideas struggle

- Public social networks are hard because they need a lot of people before they feel alive.
- Activity monitoring is trust-heavy. If it exposes too much, it starts feeling like shame or control instead of support.
- The "men only" angle works best as a private brotherhood tone for existing friends, not as a public identity gate for the whole product.
- Deep monitoring creates privacy, security, and relationship risks. The best wedge is not "see everything"; it is "see enough to reach out."
- Desktop activity and phone activity are different markets. A desktop MVP can prove the trust loop before adding mobile.

## Adjusted idea

Brotherhood should start as a small private circle for men who already trust each other.

Core rules:

- Consent first: activity is visible only after the owner allows that person.
- Directional access: I can let you see mine without automatically seeing yours.
- Revocable access: permission can be removed at any time.
- Data minimization: share app names and rough time only. No screenshots, no websites, no full URLs, no search queries, no messages.
- Encouragement over replies: notes are designed to be received without pressure to answer.
- Tiny group first: build for you and a few friends before thinking about a larger network.

## MVP included here

- Profile creation with required nickname and optional image.
- Private relay hosted by one friend on a computer.
- Circle code to join.
- Feed for things done and things to do, with optional image.
- One-way encouragement notes.
- Directional activity access requests and approvals.
- Local Windows activity summaries: current app and app time in the last hour.
